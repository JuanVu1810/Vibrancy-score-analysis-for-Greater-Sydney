Why both ends score higher hasn't been tested: dense, busy areas may simply turn up at both ends of the income range.

How far can you trust it?

A screening tool, not a footfall forecast

It's meant for councils, planners and anyone weighing up where to put lighting, crossings or a new shop. Four limits to keep in mind:

The score is relative

Every measure is standardised against these {{n_scored}} regions. Add or drop regions and every score moves.

It counts density

Measures are per km² (the polling place input uses a raw count, see the README), so small, packed regions score high almost by construction. It shows where activity is concentrated, not where the most people go.

It hasn't met real crowds

Nothing here has been compared with measured pedestrian counts. Use it to shortlist places to look at, not as evidence of footfall.

It isn't a stand-in for wealth

Score and median income are only weakly linked (correlation {{corr}}). {{low_income_name}} has the lowest median income of all {{n_scored}} regions, yet ranks #{{low_income_rank}} for score.

More limits, including our own judgement calls on the weights, are in the README.

Some score inputs are divided by the population they serve, so very small populations can produce unstable, misleadingly high values.
